# Production playbooks

Open this page when the work needs format or craft knowledge beyond what the Creation and Production
references give you. Playbooks help you direct; the Source and Run remain your authored work.

## Combine two scales

| Scale | What it describes | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Format** | a recurring whole-work relationship: who carries the argument, how attention travels, what reveal or payoff ties the piece together | podcast, ranking, short drama |
| **Craft** | one local directing problem that turns up across many formats | Caption, B-roll, continuity, image generation, sound, graphic composition |

To decide what matters right now, look at the reference's Analysis and Timeline, the target's Brief
and Treatment, and the Source as it stands. One piece may borrow from more than one format and lean on
many crafts; a hybrid or brand-new form may use craft pages and fit no format page.

The pages below hold directing knowledge that stays stable across works. If none of them fits the
question in front of you, work straight from the project's Analysis, Timeline, Brief, Treatment,
Source and visible Results. No work has to fit a playbook before you can understand or make it.

Consult each craft whose question genuinely comes up, including ones that only surface after a
Studio preview or a finished Result. Using a craft again does not mean rereading it, unless the
decision itself has changed.

When you turn those decisions into Source: footage and standalone pictures are placed, with explicit
playback, through [Visual Clips](../production/visual-clips.md);
[media preparation](../production/media.md#keep-original-and-processed-material-explicit) covers
processing source material, including transparency; [spatial layout](../production/spatial.md) owns fitting and
cropping. How these combine follows from what the material is meant to do.

## Formats

| Page | Relevant when the work involves |
| --- | --- |
| [UGC and talking-head performance](formats/talking-head.md) | one person speaking straight to the viewer, in a naturally edited performance |
| [Short drama](formats/short-drama.md) | a story told through rich A-roll: characters, how they relate, what they do, and settings that change with the drama |
| [Two-person podcast](formats/two-person-podcast.md) | two hosts seen from complementary angles, reactions in conversation, a product passed around, lifestyle coverage |
| [Street interview](formats/street-interview.md) | a motivated encounter; guest, interviewer and shared views; reveals; tracked Caption |
| [Ranking and listicle](formats/ranking-listicle.md) | a board or comparison that stays on screen and updates as the argument advances |
| [Presenter-led visual explainer](formats/presenter-led-explainer.md) | a spoken performance joined by evolving demonstrations and MG, the presenter shifting among full-frame, inset and hidden-behind-coverage |
| [Narration-led work](formats/narration-led-demo.md) | a free-standing voice that organizes products, hands, screens, demos, montage or MG |

A format page earns its place when its relationships shed light on this work. It is not a category the
project has to declare, and its examples set no limit on what the work may combine or invent.

## Then go to the owning package

Once a playbook has told you which role or relationship you need, get the exact Surfaces, ports, fields
and model options from `clipkin vocabulary <package>` and the owning package's README. Creation
references own the understanding and intention of the work; Production references own Source, Run,
preview, Build and Result behavior; the project files own the facts and decisions of this work.
