# Narration-led work

Open this page when a voice that stands on its own drives a whole work or a long passage, and the
picture comes from products, hands, screens, demonstrations, B-roll, Typography or MG.

## What organizes the passage

What organizes a narration-led passage is accepted independent speech: audio-only A-roll. The pictures
are designed around that performance; they are not stopgap coverage laid over a visible speaker.
Familiar forms: desk demos, screen tutorials, product walkthroughs, visual essays, narrated montage,
MG explainers.

**The test is not whether a face happens to be on screen.**

| Situation | What leads |
| --- | --- |
| a presenter's on-camera delivery is covered by B-roll for a few seconds | still the visible performance |
| the voice was never tied to an on-camera delivery underneath | independent narration (this page) |
| the story moves between the two | a mixed work; switch relationship when the story does |

Write down which relationship applies in the Treatment, because it decides how performance, picture
coverage, visual proof and later review should be judged. The neighboring form, where an accepted
on-camera performance stays available while demonstrations and graphics take over much of the
picture, is covered by [Presenter-led visual explainers](presenter-led-explainer.md).

## One voice, many visual answers

Put the adopted narration in the Script, and use Selections and Moments to name the meanings that need
a visual response. A single continuous passage can carry many picture changes. Cutting to another
product angle, screen state, action, example or graphic reveal usually just adds a visual answer
inside the same spoken take; it is no reason to split the take.

Choose each picture by what it contributes:

| Picture role | Job |
| --- | --- |
| **context shot** | establishes who, where, or what is being demonstrated |
| **proof shot** | lets the viewer see a claim hold: the thing in use, its state, its result, a comparison |
| **B-roll** | brings in a concrete action, object or situation, or an emotional counterpoint |
| **screen media** | puts an interface or state on screen when its precise details count |
| **MG and Typography** | make structure, build-up, change, or an idea no camera could capture, easy to grasp |

These roles can run at the same time. A device frame can stay put while the screen inside it changes,
Caption follows the narration, a label stays visible and a sound answers a UI event. Author each
relationship by what persists and what changes, instead of flattening the passage into a string of
isolated cards.

## Show proof, not a picture per noun

Every picture should move the argument forward. A speed claim wants a visibly fast action or a
shortened process. A product claim wants the product in use or its result on screen. An explanation
wants a graphic relationship that reads at a glance. Showing a generic image for each noun in the
narration creates motion without proof.

Generated images and video are ordinary production media: direct the image model as a well-informed
painter and the video model as a well-informed camera crew. Bring in exact references for a private
product, brand, person, data or interface whenever public knowledge cannot establish them. Let the
Treatment and the accepted references shape each prompt, and take the exact inputs and limits from the
selected model package's vocabulary and README.

## One narrator throughout

The narration can be an already recorded final voice track, or speech generated from an accepted Voice
Reference (by default Qwen3 `VoiceClone` lines; MiniMax `ClonedSpeech` for languages outside Qwen3's
ten or per-line emotion control); [Voice and performance](../craft/voice-and-performance.md) separates
these two uses and chooses the speech package. When you
generate fresh performances for a person who is also seen speaking in A-roll, one Voice Reference can
drive both their on-camera delivery and their off-camera narration. The sound-picture
relationship changes; who is speaking does not.

Duration estimation and alignment belong to
[Script and time](../../creation/script-and-time.md). One accepted narration performance can carry many
picture changes, and their semantic relationships follow that performance without forcing new seams
into the speech.

## Leave no gaps in picture or sound

No stretch of the work should be left without a chosen picture, whether that is a shot that carries
on, the next B-roll item, a component that stays up, a handoff or an authored background. Watch the rendered Film to confirm that no leftover or
empty edge shows between those decisions. Music, ambience and effects can tie cuts together or mark
structural turns while keeping the narration clear; [Sound and mix](../craft/sound-mix.md) owns that
relationship.

Caption is spoken text and nothing else. Steps, labels, figures, headings, CTA copy and interface
writing each belong to a Typography, Text, screen or MG role of their own, except where the viewer is
meant to read them as the narration too.

## Check that the pictures earn their place

First watch the passage as an argument: does every visual change make the words clearer, prove them,
set their pace or heighten them? Then check the details: is the product and screen content accurate, do hands and devices
have believable geometry, is Caption on time, do persistent components hold the right state, and do
the visual handoffs work?

Let the visuals build a clear progression alongside the narration. Weak proof is fixed in the shot or
component design; wrong timing is fixed at the authored semantic relation.
